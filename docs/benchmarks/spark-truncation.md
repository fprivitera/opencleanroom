# Benchmark: truncation on Spark

Generated by `packages/opencleanroom/bench/spark_bench.py` at commit `3886dcc`.

## Setup

- Data: synthetic, heavy-tailed taxi dataset built inside Spark: 20,000,000 trips, 200,000 drivers, 263 zones. The busiest driver has 341,454 trips, the median driver 55; truncation keeps k = 50. trips: 20,000,000 rows, trips materialized: 8,917,376 rows.
- Engine: Spark 4.1.3 behind Spark Connect; client on arm64 (Darwin 25.3.0).
- Spark ran in the official apache/spark:4.1.3 image, local mode, alone in Docker limited to 8 CPUs and 7.65 GiB, driver memory 5g, on an Apple M3 laptop.
- Not used: real NYC TLC data (the host does not resolve from this machine, and the public parquet files carry no driver id, so they have no privacy unit) and TPC-H (DuckDB's tpch extension cannot be downloaded here).
- Each figure is 5 timed runs after one warm-up, wall clock of the backend round trip (execute and collect); with 5 runs, p95 is the slowest run. Data built in 6 s; materialization took 15 s.
- A ratio below 1x does not mean truncation is free: the materialized table holds at most k rows per privacy unit, so it is smaller than the table the plain aggregate scans. The plain aggregate is the consumer's SQL as written, with no truncation, clamp or noise.

## Summary

- Window, on the 12 shapes with a truncated table: median overhead 24.5x, worst 59.1x.
- Materialized, on the 8 shapes that read the materialized table: median 0.7x, worst 0.7x. Target of at most 3x: met.
- The 4 shapes with a filter on `trips` keep the window: median 33.7x, worst 53.3x. Target missed for them. Filtering the materialized table after truncation would serve them too, at the cost of answers that differ from the window's; that choice is open.
- `Runtime.query` end to end (rewrite, ledger, backend, noise), `SELECT COUNT(*), SUM(fare_amount) FROM trips`, 10 runs: p50 0.05 s, p95 0.06 s.
